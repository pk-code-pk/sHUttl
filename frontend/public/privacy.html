sHUttl

Privacy

Last updated November 2026. Covers the website at shuttl.live and the sHUttl app for iPhone.

  • No account. sHUttl never asks for your name, email address, phone number or Harvard login.
  • Your location is used to answer the question you asked (which stop is nearest, how to get from here to there) and is not kept as a history.
  • Your class schedule stays on your device. Only if you switch on reminders does the next week of class times and rooms go to the sHUttl server, and each one is deleted when the class starts.
  • Schedule screenshots are read by OpenAI and are not saved by the sHUttl server.
  • No ads, no sale of data, no tracking across other apps or sites.

Who runs this

sHUttl is an independent student project. It is not affiliated with, operated by or endorsed by Harvard University. It reads the public live shuttle tracker and published timetable, and nothing about you is sent to Harvard by sHUttl.

What sHUttl handles, and why

Your location

Asked for only so the app can find the stops near you, show what is leaving from them, and plan a trip from where you are. On iPhone this is the "While Using the App" permission; the app never reads your location in the background.

  • When you use one of those features, your coordinates are sent to the sHUttl server with the request so it can work out the answer. The server does not save them to a database.
  • A planned trip's answer is cached for 15 seconds, filed under the start and end coordinates rounded to about 10 metres, so that an identical request straight afterwards is fast.
  • The one case where a location is stored: if reminders are on and a class starts within the next hour, your current location is saved with that reminder so the server can plan from where you actually are. It is deleted with the reminder when the class starts.
  • If you share a trip that starts from your location, the link you share contains those coordinates. It goes wherever you send it.

You can refuse location and pick a stop by hand instead; everything still works.

Your class schedule

The Classes tab reads your schedule from screenshots you choose. On iPhone that may involve the camera or your photo library, but only for the pictures you pick. The iPhone app asks for your permission before the first screenshot is sent, and sends nothing if you decline.

  • The screenshots are uploaded to the sHUttl server, which passes them to OpenAI to be read into a list of classes (course, section, days, times, room, term dates). The sHUttl server holds them in memory for that one request and does not write them to disk or a database.
  • OpenAI processes the images under its own API terms. OpenAI states that data sent through its API is not used to train its models, and that it may keep API requests for a limited period to monitor for abuse. Do not upload a screenshot that shows anything you would not want OpenAI to receive.
  • The resulting list of classes is stored on your device, in the app's or browser's local storage. It is not uploaded, unless you turn on reminders (next section).
  • If the reading fails, a short excerpt of the unreadable reply may be written to the server's error log.

Reminders ("Remind me")

Off unless you switch it on. When it is on, the sHUttl server stores:

  • A random install ID created on your device. It is not your Apple ID, device serial number or advertising identifier, and it is not linked to your name.
  • A notification address: on iPhone, the device token Apple issues to the app; in a browser, the push subscription your browser creates. It is what lets a notification reach you and nothing else.
  • Your classes for the next seven days: for each one its title, start time, room, and where you will be coming from (your chosen home stop, the previous class's room, or the location described above).

The server uses these to work out which shuttle gets you to each class and to send one "leave now" notification. The notification's text (class, stop, route, times) is delivered through Apple's push service on iPhone, or your browser maker's push service on the web.

Things kept only on your device

Your class list, your home stop, your light or dark map choice, whether reminders are on, and the random install ID. Deleting the app, or clearing site data for shuttl.live in a browser, removes all of it.

Website analytics

The website uses Vercel Web Analytics to count page views. According to Vercel it uses no cookies and records aggregate details such as the page, the referring site, and browser, device type and country. The iPhone app does not load it and contains no analytics or advertising code.

Server logs and your IP address

Like any service on the internet, the servers see your IP address when your device connects. The sHUttl server uses it for a few minutes to limit how many requests one address can make. The companies hosting the server and the website keep routine request logs for a limited time, and those can include the IP address and the address of the request, which for a nearby-stops or trip request includes the coordinates sent. sHUttl does not use these logs to build a profile of anyone.

Where it goes

Who What they receive Why
sHUttl server Location with a request; schedule screenshots (passed on, not kept); and, with reminders on, the install ID, notification address and upcoming classes. To plan trips, read schedules and send reminders. Run on rented cloud hosting.
OpenAI The schedule screenshots you upload. To read them into a list of classes.
Apple (iPhone app) The text of each reminder notification, addressed to your device token. Apple's push service is the only way to deliver a notification to an iPhone.
Your browser's push service (website) Each reminder notification, encrypted for your browser. The same job, for browsers. Run by the browser's maker (for example Google, Mozilla or Apple).
CARTO Requests for the pieces of map you are looking at, which come from your IP address. CARTO serves the background map. It does not receive your location, schedule or install ID from sHUttl.
Vercel (website only) The page requests themselves, and the page-view analytics described above. Vercel hosts shuttl.live.

Nobody else. Nothing is sold, rented or shared for advertising.

How long it is kept

  • Location sent with a request: not stored, beyond the 15-second trip cache and routine request logs.
  • Schedule screenshots: not stored by the sHUttl server. OpenAI's retention is set by OpenAI.
  • Reminders (class title, time, room, starting point): each is deleted automatically once that class's start time has passed, so nothing is older than about seven days. The whole set is replaced whenever your class list changes.
  • Notification address: deleted the moment you turn "Remind me" off. It is also deleted if Apple or your browser's push service reports that it no longer works, for example after the app is deleted. Otherwise it is kept for as long as reminders stay on.
  • Rate-limit counters by IP address: a few minutes.

Turning reminders off deletes your notification address straight away, so nothing further can be sent to you. Reminders already on the server are not removed at that instant; they expire on the schedule above.

Turning things off

  • Location: on iPhone, open the Settings app and find sHUttl under Privacy & Security > Location Services. In a browser, use the site's location permission. Then pick a stop by hand in the app.
  • Reminders: tap "Remind me" in the Classes tab to switch it off. You can also turn off notifications for sHUttl in iPhone Settings, or for shuttl.live in your browser.
  • Your schedule: the trash button ("Clear schedule") in the Classes tab removes it from the device.
  • Camera and photos: only used when you choose a screenshot. You can remove the permissions in iPhone Settings.
  • Everything on the device: delete the app, or clear site data for shuttl.live.

What sHUttl does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, or contact details.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No tracking of you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No sale of personal data.
  • No reading of your location in the background.

Children

sHUttl is a transit tool for a university campus. It is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If what sHUttl collects changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves.

Questions and requests

sHUttl is open source. For a question about privacy, or to ask for something to be removed, open an issue on the project's GitHub repository: github.com/pk-code-pk/sHUttl/issues. Please do not post personal details in a public issue. Because sHUttl never learns who you are, there is no way to look up "your" data by name; turning reminders off in the app is what removes your notification address from the server.
